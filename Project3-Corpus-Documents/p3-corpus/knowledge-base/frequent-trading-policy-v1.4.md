# Frequent Trading Policy

Document ID: POL-FTP
Version: 1.4
Effective: 2025-03-10
Supersedes: 1.3
Owner: Fund Operations

## 1. Why this policy exists

Frequent buying and selling of the same mutual fund raises costs for every long-term shareholder in that fund, because the fund has to trade to meet the flows. This policy limits short-term round trips.

## 2. What counts as a round trip

2.1 A **round trip** is a purchase of (or exchange into) a fund, followed by a sale of (or exchange out of) the same fund within **30 calendar days**.

2.2 The order matters. Selling first and buying back later within 30 days also counts as a round trip.

## 3. The restriction

3.1 After one round trip in a fund, the client cannot buy or exchange into that same fund for **60 calendar days**. Sales are never restricted.

3.2 The restriction applies per fund, across all of the client's accounts at the firm.

3.3 Client services and advisors cannot lift the restriction early.

## 4. Exemptions

The following do not count toward a round trip:

4.1 Purchases and sales of the Federal Money Market Fund.

4.2 Purchases made through an automatic investment plan and withdrawals made through an automatic withdrawal plan.

4.3 Rebalancing that happens inside a target retirement or balanced fund, since the client is not trading.

4.4 Reinvested dividends and capital gains.

## 5. Notice

5.1 A client who completes a round trip receives an email explaining the 60-day restriction and the date it ends.
