**What is a distribution?**
Funds pass the income and gains they earn on to shareholders as distributions. Dividends come from interest and dividends the fund receives. Capital gains distributions come from the fund selling investments for more than it paid.

**How often do funds pay distributions?**

| Fund type | Dividends | Capital gains |
|---|---|---|
| Money market and bond funds | Monthly | Annually, in December |
| U.S. stock funds | Quarterly, in March, June, September and December | Annually, in December |
| Balanced and target retirement funds | Quarterly | Annually, in December |
| International stock funds | Annually, in December | Annually, in December |
| Sector funds | Annually, in December | Annually, in December |

**What happens to my distributions by default?**
They are reinvested in more shares of the same fund, at no charge.

**Can I take distributions as cash instead?**
Yes. Go to Account, then Distribution options, and choose to have distributions sent to your bank account or put into the Federal Money Market Fund. You can choose separately for each fund.

**When does a change to my distribution option take effect?**
For any distribution with a record date at least 3 business days after you make the change. Earlier distributions follow the old option.

**Why did the fund's share price drop after a distribution?**
The distribution is paid out of the fund's assets, so the share price drops by about the amount paid. If you reinvest, you own more shares at the lower price, and the total value of your holding is unchanged by the distribution itself.

**Are distributions taxable?**
In a non-retirement account, distributions are generally taxable in the year they are paid, even if reinvested. They are reported on Form 1099-DIV. The firm does not give tax advice.
